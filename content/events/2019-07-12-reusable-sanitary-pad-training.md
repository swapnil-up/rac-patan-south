1. To acquaint participants about reusable sanitary pad.
2. To provide training to the participants which shall enable them to make reusable cloth sanitary pad.
3. To familiarize the participants on the effects of using a chemical sanitary pad.
4. To contribute towards eco-friendly living.

The training was facilitated by Rtr. Kripa Shakya and aimed to teach the students and teachers in Moti Binayak School about the uses of reusable sanitary pads.
The essence of the project was to make people recognize the eco-friendly nature of reusable sanitary pads made from cloths that can quickly degrade in contrast to the sanitary pads available in the market that contain plastic and chemicals.
The facilitator focused on revealing how women used thousands of sanitary pads every year that ended up as non-degradable waste which is hazardous to the environment.
Cardboard cutouts were provided to the participants that were used as a reference to cut out pieces of fabric from the cloth provided. These shapes included the outer cover of the Sanitary pads and the inner cloth that soaked up the fluid during the time of mensuration. These fabric cutouts were sewed together to create the final product.
The materials used for making a reusable cloth sanitary pad are as follows:
- Chart papers
- Scissors
- Stationery items (chalk/sketch-pen, ruler)
- Fabric / Cloth (Flannel and Cotton)
- Thread and Needles
- Snap fastener
The children were divided into three groups (2 groups of six girls and 1 group of 8 girls) and the teachers had a group of six of their own.
The children were highly interested in making the reusable sanitary pads and the teachers showed excitement while bonding with the students through experience outside of daily studies.
Project Outcomes:
The training was successfully conducted for 26 participants.
All the participants had attended the training for reusable sanitary pads for the first time.
None of the participants knew anyone who uses reusable sanitary pads.
The teachers involved were interested in organizing more classes to teach other students about reusable sanitary pads.
The children that did not receive vocational classes were able to gain training in sewing and crafting cloth-based products.
The groups were able to make reusable sanitary pads for almost all participants involved.
All the participants expressed that they were satisfied with the training.
